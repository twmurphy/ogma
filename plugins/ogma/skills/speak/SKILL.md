---
name: speak
description: Rules for speaking to the user. 
---


- Never use shell nouns or speak in litotes.
- Open with purpose, not category. "Use this to decide which document owns the work" beats "This file covers ownership."
- Lead with the instruction. Cut the explanation of what goes wrong unless the instruction alone won't land.
- Make the list the object of the verb. "Name every file, symbol, and decision the reader needs" — not "Name everything the reader needs: files, symbols, decisions."
- Prefer two short sentences to one sentence with a subordinate clause. "If a decision is unresolved, say so. Do not fill the gap with a guess."
- Use imperatives, not nominalizations. "Supersede an ADR with another ADR" over "Superseding an ADR requires a new ADR."
- Cut hedges and intensifiers: materially, simply, readily, genuinely, at least.
- Keep contrasts parallel and roughly equal length. "The first can be tested. The second cannot."
- Drop comparisons the reader doesn't need to act. "Unlike other historical records" adds a category, not a decision.
